# F1 — Optical Image Formation Model for Real-World SR (RealSR-style)

**Research angle:** How real camera optics forward models differ from the classical bicubic `x = Down(h * y) + n` assumption; papers that explicitly model RealSR optics; quantitative claims about PSF vs misregistration vs noise.

**Access date for all URLs:** 2026-09-13  
**Search notes:** WebSearch plugin was unavailable; sources retrieved via WebFetch of arXiv HTML/abs, GitHub, and publisher pages.

## 1) Forward models: bicubic toy vs real camera optics

### C1. Classical simulated SR uses a uniform, space-invariant bicubic degradation

- **Claim:** Most learning-based SISR is trained/evaluated with LR = bicubic downsample of HR, i.e. a simple uniform degradation.
- **Quote/paraphrase:** "LR images are generated by applying a simple and uniform degradation (i.e., bicubic downsampling) to their high-resolution (HR) counterparts. However, the degradations in real-world LR images are far more complicated."
- **Source:** Cai et al., *Toward Real-World Single Image Super-Resolution* (ICCV 2019), Abstract / §1.
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.00523 · https://arxiv.org/html/1904.00523v1
- **Confidence:** High (direct quote from paper abstract/intro).

### C2. Explicit real-camera image formation used by SupER (Köhler et al.)

- **Claim:** A physically written LR formation model separates optical PSF, sensor integration (binning), noise, and quantization.
- **Quote/paraphrase:** LR frame is modeled as  
  `Y_b = Q{ D_b{x(u)} + ε }`  
  with  
  `D_b{x(u)} = (H_sensor,b * H_optics * x)(u)`,  
  where `H_optics` is the optical PSF, `H_sensor,b` models spatial integration over `b×b` sensor pixels, `ε` is additive noise, and `Q` is quantization/discretization. They note `H_optics` does **not** depend on binning, while `H_sensor,b` does.
- **Source:** Köhler et al., *Toward Bridging the Simulated-to-Real Gap: Benchmarking Super-Resolution on Real Data* (TPAMI), §III-A “Image Formation”, Eqs. (2)–(3).
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.06420 · https://arxiv.org/html/1809.06420v2
- **Confidence:** High (explicit equations from paper HTML).

### C3. RealSR (Cai) uses a thin-lens focal-length model, not bicubic downsampling

- **Claim:** RealSR pairs are formed by changing focal length so magnification scales roughly linearly with `f` when object distance `u ≫ f`.
- **Quote/paraphrase:** Thin-lens equations `1/f = 1/u + 1/v`, magnification `M = h2/h1 = v/u`; for `u ≫ f`, `h2 ≈ (f/u) h1`. Increasing `f` records finer details on the same sensor. Scale is (theoretically) controlled by choosing `f`.
- **Source:** Cai et al. RealSR, §3.1 “Image formation by thin lens”, Eqs. (1)–(3).
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/html/1904.00523v1#S3.SS1
- **Confidence:** High (direct formulas).

### C4. Real optical degradation is space-variant (depth + DoF), not one global kernel

- **Claim:** Authentic RealSR degradation is spatially variant because the blur kernel varies with scene depth, and DoF changes with focal length.
- **Quote/paraphrase:** "the degradation is spatially variant since the blur kernel varies with the depth of content in a scene." Also: "For LR images in our RealSR dataset, the blur kernel varies with the depth in a scene and the DoF changes with the focal length."
- **Source:** Cai et al. RealSR, §1 and §4.
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/html/1904.00523v1
- **Confidence:** High (direct quote).  
- **Note vs bicubic:** This is the core physical reason RealSR rejects a single global `Down(·)`.

### C5. Changing focal length injects non-optical, non-cubic confounders

- **Claim:** Zoom-based pairing is not pure scale change: optical-center shift, scale error, exposure change, and lens distortion all appear.
- **Quote/paraphrase:** "adjusting focal length can result in many other changes in the imaging process, such as shift of optical center, variation of scaling factors, different exposure time and lens distortion."
- **Source:** Cai et al. RealSR, §1 and §3.3.
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/html/1904.00523v1
- **Confidence:** High.

### C6. Optical PSF is field-dependent (space-variant across FoV); field-constant PSF is often inadequate

- **Claim:** Standard deconvolution/restoration assumes a single PSF across the field of view; that assumption is often wrong. Spatially varying aberration/PSF correction is needed in many real systems.
- **Quote/paraphrase:** "deconvolution relies on the assumption that the point spread function is the same across the entire field-of-view. This assumption is often inadequate, but space-variant deblurring techniques generally require impractical amounts of calibration and computation."
- **Source:** Kohli et al., *Ring deconvolution microscopy: exploiting symmetry for efficient spatially varying aberration correction* (Nature Methods; arXiv 2022/2025), Abstract.
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.08928
- **Confidence:** High for the general optics claim (space-varying PSF / field aberrations). **Medium** for applying it directly to DSLR RealSR (this paper is microscopy-focused; RealSR itself argues depth/DoF space-variance rather than radial field-curvature terms).

### C7. Compact “real camera SR forward model” synthesis (researchers’ consensus)

- **Claim:** A RealSR-style forward model is more honestly written as  
  `y = Q{ D_s{ (H_{θ(u,r)} * I)_↓ + shot/read noise } } + ISP residuals`,  
  where `H_{θ(u,r)}` is a **space-variant PSF** depending on scene depth `u` and image-field position `r` (defocus, field curvature, aberrations), `D_s` is sensor sampling/integration, and ISP may add demosaic/compression. The bicubic model is a special case with a single known space-invariant kernel, no noise, and ideal downsampling.
- **Quote/paraphrase:** Synthesis from C1–C6 (especially SupER Eqs. 2–3 and RealSR §3–4).
- **Sources:** Köhler Eqs. (2)–(3); Cai §3.1/§4; Kohli space-varying PSF claim.
- **Confidence:** Medium-High as a synthesis; no single paper writes the full formula with all terms at once.

## 2) Papers that explicitly model RealSR optics / real kernels

### C8. RealSR (Cai et al., ICCV 2019) — the canonical RealSR dataset + LP-KPN

- **Claim:** Builds paired LR–HR by zoom (105/50/35/28 mm on Canon 5D3 & Nikon D810), registers pairs, and models non-uniform kernels with per-pixel kernel prediction (LP-KPN).
- **Quote/paraphrase:** Degradation kernels are “naturally non-uniform”; LP-KPN “efficiently learns per-pixel kernels.” Alignment pipeline: coarse align → central crop → fine align → discard misaligned pairs.
- **URLs:**  
  - Paper: https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.00523 · HTML: https://arxiv.org/html/1904.00523v1  
  - Code/data notes: https://github.com/csjcai/RealSR  
  - PDF mirror: https://csjcai.github.io/papers/RealSR.pdf
- **Access date:** 2026-09-13
- **Confidence:** High.

### C9. SupER / Köhler et al. — laboratory real acquisitions with hardware binning

- **Claim:** First comprehensive lab SR database of **all-real** acquisitions with pixel-wise GT: CMOS noise, real sampling at 4 resolution levels, motion, photometric conditions, HEVC compression.
- **Quote/paraphrase:** “>80k images of 14 scenes combining different facets: CMOS sensor noise, real sampling at four resolution levels, nine scene motion types, two photometric conditions, and lossy video coding.” Uses single-camera hardware binning rather than zoom or dual-camera beam-splitter, avoiding multi-camera registration error.
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.06420 · HTML https://arxiv.org/html/1809.06420v2
- **Access date:** 2026-09-13
- **Confidence:** High.

### C10. KernelGAN — explicit unknown SR-kernel estimation for real LR

- **Claim:** When the assumed downscale kernel differs from the true one, SR “significantly deteriorates”; KernelGAN estimates the image-specific SR kernel from the LR image alone via internal GAN training.
- **Quote/paraphrase:** “When the assumed downscaling kernel deviates from the true one, the performance of SR methods significantly deteriorates.” Generator learns the downscaling operator with the correct image-specific SR-kernel.
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.06581
- **Access date:** 2026-09-13
- **Confidence:** High.

### C11. Flow-based Kernel Prior (FKP) — anisotropic Gaussian kernel manifold

- **Claim:** Blind SR often assumes anisotropic Gaussians are sufficient for image SR; FKP replaces KernelGAN/Double-DIP kernel modules with a normalizing-flow prior over that manifold.
- **Quote/paraphrase:** “they fail to fully exploit the general SR kernel assumption that anisotropic Gaussian kernels are sufficient for image SR.” FKP improves kernel estimation accuracy and stability.
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.15977
- **Access date:** 2026-09-13
- **Confidence:** High for kernel-prior claim; **Medium** for how well anisotropic Gaussians match true camera PSFs (field curvature/defocus can be non-Gaussian).

### C12. MetaKernelGAN / later kernel estimation work still uses a *global* kernel

- **Claim:** Modern blind-SR kernel estimators (KernelGAN family, MetaKernelGAN, S2K, FKP) still typically estimate a single (or per-image) blur kernel, not a full space-variant optical PSF map.
- **Quote/paraphrase:** MetaKernelGAN meta-learns a “kernel-generating GAN” that better estimates “the magnitude and covariance of the kernel.”
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.07886
- **Access date:** 2026-09-13
- **Confidence:** Medium-High (this is a literature-structure observation from abstracts; many of these papers do not claim per-pixel space-variant kernels the way RealSR’s KPN does).

### C13. Classical accurate blur models (cited by RealSR)

- **Claim:** RealSR cites Efrat et al. (ICCV 2013) as evidence that authentic blur models matter relative to image priors.
- **Quote/paraphrase:** RealSR related-work cites “Accurate blur models vs. image priors in single image super-resolution” (Efrat et al., ICCV 2013) when arguing models trained on simulated data fail on real LR.
- **Primary URL (citation only; full text not fetched):** referenced as RealSR ref [12] in https://arxiv.org/html/1904.00523v1
- **Access date:** 2026-09-13
- **Confidence:** Medium (citation confirmed; primary paper text not retrieved in this session).

## 3) Quantitative claims: PSF vs misregistration vs noise

### C14. Simulated-to-real PSNR gap ≈ 2 dB (VDSR, same scene)

- **Claim:** On the same scene, VDSR on bicubic-simulated LR scores much higher than on real binning LR.
- **Quote/paraphrase:** VDSR on simulated data: **PSNR 34.46, IFC 3.93**; on real acquisitions of the same scene: **PSNR 32.46, IFC 2.63**. Authors: “simulation considerably overestimates the performance.”
- **Source:** Köhler et al., Fig. 1 / §I.
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/html/1809.06420v2
- **Access date:** 2026-09-13
- **Confidence:** High for the numbers.  
- **Attribution caveat:** This ~2 dB gap is **not** decomposed into PSF-only vs noise-only vs compression; it is the joint effect of real optics + sensor noise + sampling vs bicubic.

### C15. Bicubic-trained models can be *worse than or equal to* bicubic on RealSR

- **Claim:** On the RealSR test set, VDSR/SRResNet trained on bicubic-degraded DIV2K only match the simple bicubic interpolator; multi-degradation (MD) training gives only marginal gains.
- **Quote/paraphrase:** “the VDSR and SRResNet models trained on the simulated BD dataset can only achieve comparable performance to the simple bicubic interpolator. Training on the MD dataset brings marginal improvements over BD.”
- **Source:** Cai et al. RealSR, §5.1 (discussion of Table 2).
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/html/1904.00523v1#S5.SS1
- **Access date:** 2026-09-13
- **Confidence:** High for qualitative claim; **Medium** for exact PSNR deltas (tables were truncated in HTML extract; GitHub README later tables show e.g. bicubic ~28.63 dB at ×3 vs baseline ~30.60 dB on V3).

### C16. Architecture depth alone is a small lever vs authentic degradation modeling

- **Claim:** Going from VDSR/SRResNet to very deep RCAN (>400 layers) only improves **0.2–0.3 dB** on RealSR, far smaller than the domain-shift gap.
- **Quote/paraphrase:** “Employing a deeper architecture, the RCAN (>400 layers) can improve (0.2dB ~ 0.3dB) the performance over VDSR and SRResNet on all cases.”
- **Source:** Cai et al. RealSR, §5.1.
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/html/1904.00523v1
- **Access date:** 2026-09-13
- **Confidence:** High (explicit number).

### C17. Misregistration is a first-class error source in RealSR construction

- **Claim:** Without careful registration, pairs are unusable; RealSR discards misaligned pairs after progressive alignment because optical center, scale, exposure, and distortion all shift under zoom.
- **Quote/paraphrase:** “it is difficult to obtain pixel-wise aligned image pairs because the zooming of lens brings many uncontrollable changes… none of the existing image registration algorithms can be directly used.” Pipeline: PhotoShop distortion correction → center crop → iterative affine + luminance (`α, β`) estimation via robust Lp / IRLS → discard misaligned pairs.
- **Source:** Cai et al. RealSR, §3.3 and GitHub Alignment pipeline note.
- **URLs:** https://arxiv.org/html/1904.00523v1 · https://github.com/csjcai/RealSR
- **Access date:** 2026-09-13
- **Confidence:** High.  
- **Quantitative gap:** The paper does **not** report “X dB lost per pixel of misalignment.” Treat misregistration as a qualitative/structural error source that invalidates full-reference training if untreated.

### C18. Noise is explicitly a real-vs-sim axis (SupER)

- **Claim:** Real CMOS noise (and low-light / photometric variation) is part of the simulated-to-real gap; VDSR shows “noise breakthroughs and artifacts at fine structures” on real data.
- **Quote/paraphrase:** Real data suffers from “physically true image noise, low-light exposures, or photometric variations” and “non-Gaussian noise,” causing noise breakthroughs not seen in bicubic sims.
- **Source:** Köhler et al., §I / §II-A.
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/html/1809.06420v2
- **Access date:** 2026-09-13
- **Confidence:** High for existence of the effect; **Medium** for a clean “percent of error from noise” figure (none given).

### C19. No clean published decomposition “%PSF vs %misregistration vs %noise”

- **Claim:** Across RealSR (Cai), SupER (Köhler), KernelGAN, and space-varying PSF work, we did **not** find a single paper that attributes a numeric share of RealSR residual error to PSF mismatch vs alignment error vs sensor noise.
- **Evidence:** RealSR reports domain-level PSNR tables and qualitative kernel non-uniformity; SupER reports factor-isolated benchmarks (compression, motion, photometric) but not a PSF-only ablation; KernelGAN quantifies kernel mismatch impact qualitatively (“significantly deteriorates”) without a three-way error budget.
- **Sources:** all of C14–C18.
- **Confidence:** Medium-High (negative literature finding; WebSearch was down, so this is based on targeted arXiv fetches, not exhaustive search).

### C20. What *is* quantified (error budget approximation from available numbers)

| Factor | Evidence type | Approx. magnitude | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Domain gap (joint optics+noise+ISP vs bicubic) | Same-scene PSNR | ~2.0 dB (34.46 → 32.46) | Köhler Fig. 1 |
| Training on simulated BD vs authentic RealSR | Model quality on RealSR | BD-trained models ≈ bicubic baseline; RealSR-trained much better | Cai §5.1 |
| Extra depth (RCAN vs VDSR/SRResNet) | Architecture ablation | +0.2–0.3 dB | Cai §5.1 |

- **Confidence:** Medium (numbers are solid; attribution across factors is incomplete in literature).

## 4) Bottom-line model comparison (for the parent research write-up)

| Term | Bicubic sim (`x = Down(h*y)+n`) | RealSR-style optical model |
|---|---|---|
| Blur `h` | Fixed known bicubic / isotropic Gaussian | Unknown; depth- and field-dependent PSF `H_{θ(u,r)}` |
| Space variance | None | Yes (depth/DoF; field aberrations possible) |
| Sampling | Ideal decimation | Sensor pitch + optional hardware binning `H_sensor,b` |
| Noise | Often AWGN or none | Shot/read, ISO-dependent, non-Gaussian |
| Geometry | Exact scale | Zoom optical-center shift, scale error, lens distortion |
| Exposure/ISP | Ignored | Exposure change, demosaic, compression (SupER HEVC) |
| Pair alignment | Trivial | Non-trivial; misaligned pairs discarded |

**Practical implication:** Training on bicubic (or even isotropic Gaussian) pairs systematically mis-specifies the inverse problem for real camera images. The physically relevant target is a **space-variant, unknown kernel + noise + registration-error** forward model; methods that only improve network depth recover little of this gap (C16).
