# F2 — Ill-posedness and identifiability of real paired SR under residual misalignment

**Research date:** 2026-09-13  
**Scope:** Why pixel losses fail on residual misregistration; which losses/theories restore identifiability; when uncertainty weighting helps vs. merely rescales.

---

## 1. Fourier phase vs amplitude under shift; why L1 pixel loss fails under misregistration

### C1. Spatial shift multiplies Fourier phase by a linear exponential; amplitude is invariant
- **Claim:** Fourier shift theorem: \(f(x-x_0) \Leftrightarrow F(\omega)\,e^{-j\omega x_0}\). The magnitude \(|F(\omega)|\) is independent of translation; the phase carries spatial position. Reconstructing from phase alone recovers structure (up to scale); amplitude alone does not pin geometry.
- **Sources:**  
  - Oppenheim & Lim, “The importance of phase in signals,” Proc. IEEE, 1981 (classic). https://doi.org/10.1109/PROC.1981.12022  
  - Applied in modern DA: FDA swaps low-frequency **amplitude** (style/domain) while keeping phase; shows amplitude ≈ appearance, phase ≈ structure/position. https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.05498 (Yang & Soatto, CVPR 2021)  
  - Related shift/phase-invariance observation in FRFT: https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.00365
- **Date:** 1981 / 2020–2021
- **Confidence:** **high** for the theorem and amplitude/phase roles; **medium** for how strongly “amplitude-only loss” is proven sufficient in SR (used more as inductive bias than full identifiability theorem).

### C2. Residual misalignment makes the LR→HR map non-identifiable under pixel losses
- **Claim:** Real paired LR–HR (focal-length zoom, dual-camera, multi-frame) are only approximately registered. Residual shift/warp \(\delta\) means the training target is \(y = T_\delta(H(x^\star))\), not the aligned HR of \(x\). Pixel L1/L2 then supervise *wrong correspondences*. Because multiple \((H,\delta)\) explain the same LR, the inverse is **ill-posed / non-identifiable**; the Bayes-optimal predictor under expectation losses averages over the multi-modal posterior over alignments and content → **blur** (high-frequency detail is not uniquely determined).
- **Sources:**  
  - RealSR (Cai et al., ICCV 2019): builds camera-zoom paired dataset; needs progressive image **registration**; residual non-uniformity remains. https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.00523  
  - SR-RAW / CoBi (Zhang et al., CVPR 2019): explicitly states key barrier is missing aligned GT; CoBi delivers “critical robustness to **mild misalignment**” in input–output pairs. https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.05169  
  - Contextual Loss paper states the general problem: common losses “assume that these images are spatially aligned and compare pixels at corresponding locations,” but aligned pairs are often unavailable. https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.02077
- **Date:** 2018–2019
- **Confidence:** **high** (multiple real-SR papers document misalignment and the need for alignment-robust objectives).

### C3. Perception–distortion tradeoff formalizes “distortion-optimal ⇒ blur” under multi-modal posteriors
- **Claim:** Blau & Michaeli prove that lower average distortion forces worse perceptual quality for **any** distortion metric. Multi-modal posteriors (here: content + unknown alignment) make the distortion-optimal estimator a conditional average/median that blurs; GAN/adversarial or distributional objectives trade distortion for sharpness. This is the theoretical backbone of “L1/L2 on misaligned pairs blurs.”
- **Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.06077 (CVPR 2018)
- **Date:** 2017–2018
- **Confidence:** **high** for the general theorem; **high** that it applies to misregistration-induced multi-modality; **medium** on quantitative SR-specific corollaries (the paper is general restoration, not SR-alignment-specific).

### C4. Networks are not shift-invariant; sub-pixel input shift changes outputs drastically
- **Claim:** Strided conv / max-pool violate sampling theory; small translations cause large output changes. So even tiny residual misregistration is not “noise”—it changes the function the net must fit, encouraging averaged/blurry solutions and unstable training.
- **Source:** Zhang, “Making Convolutional Networks Shift-Invariant Again,” ICML 2019. https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.11486
- **Date:** 2019
- **Confidence:** **high** for classification CNNs; **medium** as a direct SR-identifiability claim (mechanism transfers; not an SR paper).

### C5. L1 vs L2 under unknown shift (synthesized theory)
- **Claim:**  
  - **L2:** optimum is \(E[y\mid x]\); averaging over random \(\delta\) ≈ convolution with the shift kernel ⇒ **blur**.  
  - **L1:** optimum is the conditional median; still blurry when the conditional is multi-modal in pixel space (median of shifted copies ≠ a sharp mode).  
  - Therefore switching L2→L1 does **not** restore identifiability under misregistration; it only softens outliers. Robust M-estimators (Huber/Charbonnier) reduce gradient blow-up on misaligned pixels but leave the multi-modal average intact.
- **Sources:** synthesis of C2–C3 + standard robust-loss literature (e.g. Barron, CVPR 2019, “A General and Adaptive Robust Loss Function,” https://arxiv.org/abs/1701.10157 — not fetched live this session; **do not treat as verified URL**).
- **Date:** 2026-09-13 (synthesis)
- **Confidence:** **high** on L2-blur; **medium–high** on L1 under multi-modal misalignment (standard statistical argument; less often stated in one SR paper).

## 2. Losses / theory that restore identifiability (or alignment-invariance)

### C6. Contextual Loss: nearest-neighbor matching in deep feature space (no alignment required)
- **Claim:** CX compares each output patch to the nearest target patch in VGG feature space with relative distances and image-context weighting. It is **invariant to spatial layout** of corresponding content (eyes→eyes without pixel alignment). Restores *content* correspondence when pixel correspondences fail; does **not** pin absolute geometry (can transfer structure at wrong places if used alone).
- **Source:** Mechrez, Talmi, Zelnik-Manor, ECCV 2018. https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.02077
- **Date:** 2018
- **Confidence:** **high** for non-aligned training; **medium** for “identifiability” in a strict inverse-problem sense (it is a matching heuristic, not a uniqueness proof).

### C7. Contextual Bilateral Loss (CoBi): CX + spatial range constraint
- **Claim:** CoBi adds a **bilateral** spatial-range term so matches stay local (critical for real zoom SR with mild misalignment). On SR-RAW, CoBi is the objective that makes real paired training work despite residual misregistration—direct evidence that alignment-robust features restore useful gradients under real capture noise.
- **Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.05169 (CVPR 2019)
- **Date:** 2019
- **Confidence:** **high** (paper’s stated contribution and ablations).

### C8. Amplitude-only / frequency-magnitude losses are shift-invariant by construction
- **Claim:** Because \(|F|\) is shift-invariant (C1), an amplitude-only objective cannot punish a correct structure at the wrong global translation—so it is **immune to pure translation misregistration**. Phase-informed or complex-spectrum losses (e.g. Focal Frequency Loss) still need alignment for phase terms. Practical recipe for residual shift: prefer magnitude-spectrum + local/contextual feature losses; treat phase-sensitive pixel losses as weak or gated.
- **Sources:**  
  - Shift theorem (C1)  
  - Focal Frequency Loss (complex spectrum, adaptive weighting): https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.12821 (ICCV 2021)  
  - FDA amplitude/phase split: https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.05498
- **Date:** 2020–2021
- **Confidence:** **high** on invariance of magnitude; **medium** that pure amplitude loss alone is enough for SR quality (loses geometric anchoring).

### C9. Geometry / cycle / contrastive content constraints for unpaired or weakly paired translation
- **Claim:** When pairing is untrustworthy, identifiability is restored by extra invariances:  
  - **Cycle consistency** (implicit in CycleGAN lineage) restricts the solution set.  
  - **Geometry-consistency (GcGAN):** \(G(Tx) \approx T G(x)\) for known geometric \(T\)—reduces unpaired mapping ambiguity. https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.05852  
  - **PatchNCE / CUT:** maximize mutual information between corresponding input/output patches in feature space; content preserved without pixel alignment. https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.15651 (ECCV 2020)
- **Date:** 2018–2020
- **Confidence:** **high** for unpaired I2I; **medium** for direct application to real paired SR with residual (not zero) alignment—useful regularizers, not a substitute for all paired signal.

### C10. Optimal transport couples unpaired / partially paired domains
- **Claim:** OT recovers a soft correspondence (coupling) between LR and HR distributions when hard pixel pairing is invalid. OT-guided conditional diffusion shows gains on **unpaired super-resolution** by replacing missing pairs with OT couplings.
- **Source:** Gu et al., “Optimal Transport-Guided Conditional Score-Based Diffusion Models,” NeurIPS 2023. https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.01226  
- Related extremal OT for keeping translated image close to input: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.12874
- **Date:** 2023
- **Confidence:** **high** that OT addresses unpaired coupling; **medium** for residual-misaligned *paired* SR (OT matches marginals more than fine local phase).

### C11. Operational summary: what each family identifies
| Family | Shift-invariant? | Identifies content | Identifies geometry | Failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pixel L1/L2 | No | Only if aligned | Yes if aligned | Blur under residual \(\delta\) |
| Contextual / CoBi | Mostly (CX); local in CoBi | Yes (semantic NN) | Weak / range-limited | Hallucinated placement |
| Amplitude-only Fourier | Yes | Spectrum yes | No (phase free) | Soft / misplaced detail |
| Full spectrum (FFL) | No (phase sensitive) | Yes if aligned | Yes if aligned | Needs alignment |
| Cycle / GcGAN / PatchNCE | By constraint | Partial | Partial | Mode collapse / wrong maps |
| OT coupling | Distribution-level | Marginal content | Soft transport | Coarse for sub-pixel |

- **Date:** 2026-09-13 (synthesis)
- **Confidence:** **medium–high** (consistent with sources above).

## 3. When uncertainty weighting helps vs. when it just scales the loss

### C12. Homoscedastic uncertainty weighting (Kendall et al.)
- **Claim:** For multi-task regression/classification, learn task noise \(\sigma_k\) and use \(L = \sum_k \frac{1}{2\sigma_k^2} L_k + \log\sigma_k\). Designed to handle **different units/scales** across tasks without hand tuning; can beat separately trained models.
- **Source:** Kendall, Gal, Cipolla, CVPR 2018. https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.07115
- **Date:** 2017–2018
- **Confidence:** **high** for multi-task scene geometry/semantics; **medium** for SR-specific residual terms.

### C13. Uncertainty weighting often degenerates to a scalar rescale / implicit regularizer
- **Claim:** Large-scale studies find specialized multi-task optimizers (including uncertainty-weighted objectives) **do not beat** a well-tuned weighted average; benefits often match regularization. Unitary scalarization with standard stabilization matches or improves complex MTO methods.
- **Sources:**  
  - Kurin et al., NeurIPS 2022, “In Defense of the Unitary Scalarization…” https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.04122  
  - Xin et al., 2022, “Do Current Multi-Task Optimization Methods in Deep Learning Even Help?” https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.11379
- **Date:** 2022
- **Confidence:** **high** that “just scales / regularizes” is a common outcome; **high** that careful baselines are mandatory.

### C14. When it actually helps in real SR with residual misalignment
- **Claim:** Uncertainty weighting **helps** only if the uncertainty model can express the *structure* of the error:
  1. **Heterogeneous tasks / units** (e.g. pixel + perceptual + GAN + frequency terms with different magnitudes) — a learned global \(\sigma_k\) is a cheap temperature per term (C12).
  2. **Spatially varying, content-dependent misalignment** — requires a **per-pixel (or per-patch) variance map**, not a single scalar. A map can down-weight likely-misaligned regions, acting as soft alignment / robust reweighting.
  3. **It does not help identifiability** if it only multiplies the whole L1 by a constant: that is equivalent to LR scaling and cannot un-blur a multi-modal average (C3, C5).
  4. **Risk:** a free variance head can inflate \(\sigma\) on hard textures (edges, fine detail) to reduce NLL, **suppressing exactly the frequencies SR needs** — learned soft-labeling of hard samples, not better recovery.
- **Sources:** synthesis of C12–C13 + CoBi/CX evidence that the fix is *matching structure*, not weighting (C6–C7).
- **Date:** 2026-09-13 (synthesis)
- **Confidence:** **high** on “global scalar ≠ fix for misregistration”; **medium–high** on per-pixel variance maps (principled, but easy to game; no single paper in this fetch proves it recovers identifiable SR).

### C15. Practical decision rule
- **Use uncertainty weighting** when combining *heterogeneous* losses (pixel + CX + amplitude + adversarial) with different natural scales, or when you have a *well-calibrated* spatial uncertainty map from registration (e.g. optical-flow residual).
- **Do not rely on it** as the primary cure for residual misalignment. Prefer: (i) better alignment / soft correspondence (CoBi, CX, OT); (ii) shift-invariant terms (magnitude spectrum); (iii) geometry constraints; (iv) adversarial/perceptual terms to escape the distortion-optimal blur (C3).
- **Date:** 2026-09-13
- **Confidence:** **high** as engineering synthesis from C1–C14.

## Takeaways for RealSR / essence work

1. **Ill-posedness is structural:** residual \(\delta\) + multi-modal \(p(\mathrm{HR},\delta\mid\mathrm{LR})\) ⇒ any pixel-averaging loss blurs (C2–C5).
2. **Fourier geometry fact:** amplitude is the wrong place to put alignment-sensitive supervision *or* the right place if you want invariance; phase is where shift lives (C1, C8).
3. **Restoring utility of pairs:** CX/CoBi-style local matching + optional amplitude terms + geometry constraints; OT if pairs are mostly untrustworthy (C6–C11).
4. **Uncertainty weights are second-order:** they rebalance terms; they do not create correspondence. Per-pixel maps can soft-align but risk killing texture (C12–C15).
